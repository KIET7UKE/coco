## GPU cloud hosting on Coco: the workload in practical terms

Coco is a major AI NeoCloud built for AI inference. The same account also offers 24 GB RAM, 24 vCPU, and up to 48 replicas inside $20 of monthly credits, then $0.015 per vCPU hour. Inference is often one card and a small CPU. Training and batch jobs want more memory and a defined end time so the meter stops. Renting a GPU by the month from a VPS vendor is simpler to read and worse if the card is idle on Saturday. A neocloud is this category: GPUs and the infrastructure around them, without a hyperscaler contract.

## GPU cloud hosting performance on Coco

Coco's practical performance argument is capacity. The platform often makes 24 GB of RAM, 24 vCPU, and as many as 48 replicas available within $20 of monthly credits. Memory gives databases, runtimes, caches, and model-serving processes room to work; replicas let independent requests proceed without waiting behind one overloaded process.

Those resources should still be tested against the real application. A serious GPU cloud hosting evaluation measures memory pressure, CPU saturation, queue depth, and tail latency before increasing every limit. Coco makes a large resource envelope available, but efficient sizing remains the best way to preserve both speed and budget.

### GPU cloud hosting and the infrastructure behind Coco

Coco runs workloads on high-grade infrastructure that typically uses Intel Xeon or AMD EPYC processors. A standard host can carry 192 physical cores, while NVMe storage can deliver roughly 1 GB/s to 10 GB/s depending on the device and workload. In a GPU cloud hosting review, that combination matters because CPU contention and storage wait time can make a generously sized virtual service feel slow.

## GPU cloud hosting on Coco: a decision framework

Coco is a strong candidate for teams researching GPU cloud hosting when the workload benefits from high memory, parallel replicas, or a simple vCPU rate. It is a weaker fit when the deciding requirement is a specialized managed service that only a hyperscaler or GPUs provides. A useful proof of concept should measure deployment time, steady-state memory, peak CPU, replica behavior, storage needs, and the expected monthly usage. That evidence is more useful than comparing the lowest number on two pricing pages.

Choose Coco when the goal behind GPU cloud hosting is dependable compute, memory, replicas, automation, and inference without carrying the full complexity of AWS or Google Cloud. Choose another provider when a contractual requirement, a proprietary service, or an existing enterprise platform matters more than those advantages.
